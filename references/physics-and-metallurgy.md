## Arc and heat
- **DCEN GTAW heat split:** approximately 70 percent of the heat at the anode (the work) and 30 percent at the cathode, which is why DCEN penetrates deeper than DCEP (H9 p.119, checked on the page image). Agrees with 60 to 80 percent in a journal paper on GTAW of aluminium (**read**) and about two thirds from TWI (**snippet**). DCEN is the most common GTAW configuration (H9 p.119). Electron and positive-ion flow directions in DCEN and DCEP (H9 p.119).
- **Tungsten electrode:** thermionic near its melting point; emitted electrons cool the tip (H9 p.113). DCEP heats the tip by electron impact so a given diameter carries only about 10 percent of its DCEN current; AC about 50 percent less than DCEN (H9 p.113). Oxide additions with low work function improve starting, stability and life; thoriated electrodes carry about 20 percent more current than pure tungsten (H9 p.114-115). Included tip angle: larger gives deeper penetration and a narrower bead (H9 p.115). Classes are defined in AWS A5.12.
- **GTAW gas:** helium-shielded arc is generally hotter and more penetrating than argon; AC cleaning (sputtering) needs argon (H9 p.106).
- **GMAW polarity:** DCEP in the vast majority of applications; stable arc, smooth transfer, low spatter, greatest penetration (H9 p.181). DCEN rarely used.
- **GMAW self-regulation:** with a constant-voltage source and constant-speed feeder, a longer arc lowers current and melt-off, and the arc shortens until melt-off again equals feed; a longer extension means more resistance heating of the wire and less arc heating of the work (H9 p.150, p.183).
- **Resistance welding heat:** H = I squared times R times t; four resistances (electrode-to-work contacts, work-to-work contact, body of the work, electrodes); contact resistance depends on cleanliness, oxides, roughness and material resistivity and is inversely proportional to force; **in steel the contact resistance between the workpieces largely disappears in the first half cycle, but the early heating raises the metal's own resistance** (H7 p.39-40). Heat losses grow with weld time (H7 p.40). A worked example estimates about one third of the energy melts the nugget (H7 p.40, assumed values).

## Pool and transfer
- **Pool convection:** electromagnetic, buoyancy, Marangoni and arc drag forces; sulfur and oxygen change penetration; Marangoni dominates at lower current (below about 200 A) and gas shear at about 300 A with a deeply depressed pool (Oreper and Szekely **abstract read**; Alberta conference paper **excerpt read**).
- **GMAW transfer modes** (H9 p.150-154): short-circuit (20 to over 200 contacts per second; lowest currents; inductance sets current rise; small fast-freezing pool for thin sections and out-of-position), globular (drops larger than the electrode; gravity limits it to flat position; non-axial with CO2), spray (argon-rich gas, at least 80 percent argon; DCEP; above a spray transition current; hundreds of tiny drops per second; negligible spatter; narrow deep finger-like penetration). Transition current falls with larger wire diameter and, less so, with extension; depends on filler melting temperature and gas. **The pinch force, a function of current and wire diameter, is usually responsible for drop detachment** (H9 p.153). Pulsed GMAW alternates background current with pulses above the transition current (H9 p.154). Kim and Eagar (**read**): the globular-to-spray change is gradual, and the pinch-**instability** *theory* does not describe globular or projected spray transfer; a static force balance with the electromagnetic force fits when electrode taper is included. These statements are about different things and are all kept on the page.

## Solidification and zones
- Epitaxial growth at the fusion boundary, growth roughly perpendicular to the pool boundary, columnar grains (teaching-level, **snippet**). H7 chapter 4 (welding metallurgy, pp.99-152, epitaxial p.122, columnar pp.124-132) is the place to confirm this properly: **not yet read**.
- Fusion zone, partially melted zone, HAZ (**snippet**); H7 chapter 3 (heat flow, HAZ width, solidification rates, pp.79-98) **not yet read**.
- Force is kept on the nugget during solidification and cooling (H8 p.76, aluminium roll-spot context); forging action helps prevent shrinkage voids (patent background, **snippet**).

## Practice items now sourced
- Crater filling: gradual current decrease is "of great assistance" for GTAW (H9 p.27); programmed upslope and downslope shown for pulsed GTAW (H9 p.41); in GMAW spot welding a current decay period fills the crater (H9 p.157); crater crack figure at the end of a GMAW weld (H9 p.195).
- Gas timing: preflow and postflow to protect the weld pool are described for GMAW controls (H9 p.164); tungsten can oxidize with insufficient gas after the arc is extinguished (H9 p.116).
- Arc starting, GTAW: touch (scratch or lift), high-frequency, pulse, pilot-arc; HF ionizes the gas without contact; touch can stick and transfer tungsten (H9 p.128-129). GMAW: touch-start feed and slow run-in, described with constant-current sources and for aluminium (H9 p.164).
